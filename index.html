    Population: Our World in Data, “Population – HYDE, Gapminder, UN – Long-run data” (HYDE 3.3 to 1799, Gapminder 1800–1949, UN WPP 2024 from 1950), CC BY 4.0. Values use present-day borders; points between source years (1500, 1600, 1700, then every decade to 1800) are log-interpolated, and the series can jump where sources change (1800, 1950). Pre-contact estimates are highly uncertain (scholarly range ≈ 8–110 million for the hemisphere).

    Voyages: Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database (2019) and Intra-American Slave Trade Database 1.0, SlaveVoyages.org, CC BY-NC 3.0; annual totals from the SlaveVoyages estimates. Ships are drawn from the main place of purchase to the main place of landing, timed to the year of arrival. Coordinates from the SlaveVoyages geographic tables. Columbus routes are approximate.

    Free migration: US DHS Yearbook 2019 and Statistics Canada A350 totals, plus historical estimates. Ships and sailing paths are schematic, not individual voyage records. Colonial Spanish aggregate estimates are rotated evenly across historically available Atlantic gateways in the Caribbean, Mexico, northern South America, Panama, and (from the broader 1778 trade opening) the Río de la Plata. This distribution is illustrative, not measured migrant shares for each destination. Peru-bound journeys via Nombre de Dios/Portobelo stop at the Atlantic gateway; onward isthmus crossings and Pacific voyages are not drawn. Earlier restricted Buenos Aires traffic and later Cape Horn routes are not included in this gateway model. Route history: UNESCO and Boyd-Bowman’s recorded emigrant samples, which are not a complete destination-share series through 1820. British colonial estimates retain selected ports by period.
